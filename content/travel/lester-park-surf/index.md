So I surfed Stoney Point a couple weeks ago and just yesterday, I surfed [Lester
Park][]. The surf conditions were looking solid, hurricane Isaias in Florida
must have pushed enough hot air north that there was need for correction. Well,
what do you know, there was quite the wind patten up in Hudson Bay that was
shooting some Canadian jet streams south towards Superior for a solid day of
14-18 knot conditions all day. So, last minute on Sunday, I packed up the car
and drove the 2.5 hours north to Duluth and camped overnight right on the lake.

{{< img src="norshore.gif" alt="Lester Park Break" class="img-right" >}}

When I parked the car to sleep, it was around 80°F and by the time 3AM rolled
around (I brought the sleeping pad with a hole in it, RIP), I had to close the
sunroof since it was about 45°F outside and the wind was howling. So, I knew I
had made just about the right move. The sun didn't rise until 7:20AM that
morning, and it was quite cloudy, so I had to go work a bit at a coffee shop and
delay paddling out until lunch.

Essentially the minute 11AM hit, I was out of Dunn Bros and driving to Lester to
scope the spot. Now, when I went to Lester a couple weeks ago, the conditions
were perfect for longboarding. This time? I wish I would have brought my 6'6".
The extra buoyancy and being able to fit the actual board in the wave would have
been nice. I caught my nose a couple times and the extra surface area really
forced me to paddle against the rip current pretty much the whole session. The
thing about these lake waves is that there isn't a perfect spot to sit since
it's all wind swell. Oh and the swell period? Absolutely brutal. You really have
to time when you walk out since you'll get swallowed up by wave after wave and
the rip will just pull you back in.

Anyways, once you did find a wave you wanted, and you were relatively in
position, you have to act fast. This was another reason the longboard was not
the move yesterday. Usually, to catch a wave quickly on it, you sit back on the
tail and spin around. But with the wind variably coming from pretty much every
direction, the board would catch the wind and throw me off balance, making my
kook ass miss the wave I'd just spent 10mins paddling for (the video was short
during a tame period).

So what did I learn? Always pack the fun board, be prepared for harsher, rocky
conditions (goodbye sandy beaches), and keep the paddling endurance up. You've
gotta fight hard for every wave up north!
