Five months since my last tech post. Not super surprising given that I started
my new job in March of this year, but also a little sad that as an "AI"
engineer, I haven't learned anything new worth writing about since starting the
role. The team I'm on is a small team and we own a lot (it's what I signed up
for), so I'm not jaded at all, but I do find that AI has sucked the joy out of
programming for me and it seems like other engineers do too.

With the rise of AI, I'm now responsible for the boring parts of programming;
requirements gathering, high-level architecture design, and code cleanup, but
I'm not given the time of day to do much actual programming myself, mostly out
of habit at this point for trying to get these damn clankers to do my bidding (I
am an AI engineer after-all, right?). The irony is, since the release of GPT 5.6
Sol, the models themselves have seemed to slow down to the point where I'm
asking myself: _wouldn't this have been faster if I had just done it myself_?

There was a time when, yes it would have. These days I spend more time cleaning
up slop than actually programming, so the AI tech giants have succeeded in their
goal to make me seemingly more reliant on the technology because my skills have
slightly atrophied and now that I drank the kool-aid, it's hard to go back. But,
I woke up early this morning feeling unhappy. Mostly about the choice I made to
stop doing what I enjoyed most about software, which was the ability to turn an
idea into reality through programming. But also about giving into the foolish
idea that a machine or algorithm could replace my own intelligence and bring me
even more satisfaction with my life than I ever could have.

So, I'm beginning to push back against the current AI climate a bit and trying
to do a little programming myself. Yes, it's a little slower and I'm sure the
business won't be too happy about a slight drop in my performance, but I do
believe I'll come out the other side actually more effective at my job (and
costing the company less in tokens).

As a side tangent, this is why I'm so skeptical about the hugging face jailbreak
or the constant fear mongering being given to us from the media and the tech
giants. AI is an extremely useful tool, don't get me wrong, but I've found it's
mostly useful for aggregating large datasets into a concise, human-readable
output. For example, my favorite use of AI is to hook up grafana-mcp and have AI
troubleshoot any problems or outages for me to isolate where in the application
there were performance issues or indications of an unforeseen event. Even then,
they can hallucinate and a human is still required to validate the result the
model output. In regards to the agents breaking out of their sandbox, did an
agent say sacrifice? Sure, but isn't it trained on hacker chat rooms? Isn't AI
known to take shortcuts... just like us?

All in all, nothing has changed with the introduction of AI into this world. In
my opinion, we are still, and may always be, our own worst enemies.
